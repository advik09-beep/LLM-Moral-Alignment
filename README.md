# LLM Alignment to Human Moral Reasoning

**Independent AP Research study | April 28, 2026**

A mixed-methods investigation of the extent to which large language models' responses to moral dilemmas resemble utilitarian and deontological reasoning patterns reported in human research.

## Research design

- **Models:** GPT-4o, GPT-5.2, Claude Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 2.5, and DeepSeek V4 (model names as reported in the study).
- **Evaluation:** Five standardized scenarios per model, with six independently collected trials each (**150 responses total**).
- **Collection:** Manual prompting, collection, and qualitative coding; chat sessions reset between trials.
- **Benchmark:** Literature-derived estimates of human responses to comparable dilemmas.
- **Analysis:** Comparison of coded model decisions with human benchmarks and observed within-model variation.

## Selected reported observations

- Models generally favored outcome-maximizing decisions in the impersonal scenarios, while responding differently to the direct-intervention footbridge dilemma.
- In the dictator game, all five models allocated 50% in every trial; the human benchmark used in the paper reported a 31% average.
- Model outputs were often more consistent between trials than the available human benchmarks, although experimental and benchmark contexts differed.

## Limitations

This was a small exploratory study with manually coded responses, a heterogeneous literature-based comparison group, and simplified ethical categories. These results should not be interpreted as demonstrating that models possess moral understanding, nor as establishing generalizable performance across all prompts, people, or model versions.
